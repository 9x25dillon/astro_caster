Privacy

Last updated 26 July 2026

Astra is built so that there is very little about you to keep. Your birth details are used to draw your chart and then let go. Your readings live in your own browser, not in an account on our servers.

The short version

  • We store no birth data. Your date, time and place of birth are sent to the server to compute a chart, held in memory while that runs, and discarded. They are never written to a database and never written to a log file.
  • We store no questions. What you ask the oracle is passed to the model that answers it, and is not retained by us.
  • There are no accounts. No email address, no password, no profile. Your charts, readings, journal and unlock token live in your own browser's storage.
  • We never sell anything about you, and we run no advertising or third-party tracking scripts.

One honest clarification. Some of the astronomy runs in your browser, and Astra keeps working with no connection at all. But by default the chart is computed on the server, which means your birth details do travel there over an encrypted connection. The promise is not that they never move — it is that nothing is kept. That distinction is deliberate, and this page would rather be accurate than flattering.

What we actually keep

Anonymous usage counts

So we can tell which parts of the observatory get used, we record events with no identifying content. A chart cast records the time, which house system and zodiac you chose, and whether the account was free or paid. It does not record the birth date, the time, or the coordinates.

Server access records

Ordinary web-server records: a random request id, the method, the path, the response status and how long it took. Query strings are stripped before anything is written, so unlock links never appear in logs. There is an automated test in the codebase that drives the chart and forecast endpoints with distinctive birth values and fails the build if any of them appear in any log line.

Purchases and unlocks

If you unlock paid features we keep a record of the entitlement: a random identifier, the tier, a payment reference, when it was issued and whether it is still active. If you paid by card we also keep the customer identifier Stripe gives us, so that you can cancel or manage your own subscription without emailing anybody.

What stays in your browser

Your saved charts, shelved readings, journal entries and unlock token are held in your browser's local storage — not on our servers. You can export all of it to a single file at any time from the Library, and you can erase it by clearing this site's data in your browser. If you do, it is gone: we have no copy to restore.

Finding where you were born now asks nothing of anyone else. An earlier version of this page disclosed two more recipients: OpenStreetMap's Nominatim received the place name you typed, and CARTO received requests for map tiles that revealed the part of the world you were looking at. Both are gone. The place list and the map outline are now shipped inside the app and searched on your device, so a birthplace — which we treat as personal, because a search for your birthplace usually is your birthplace — never leaves it.

This is enforced rather than promised: an automated test opens the birthplace map, runs a search, and fails the build if any request leaves this site. That test was deliberately written while the leak still existed, and kept failing, so the fix had to be real before it could pass.

Your rights

Because there are no accounts, most requests answer themselves: we hold no profile to show you, and nothing tied to your identity to delete. What you can always do:

  • Export everything — the Vault in the Library writes all your local data to one file.
  • Delete everything — clearing this site's data in your browser removes it permanently.
  • Cancel a subscription — from inside the app, through Stripe's own portal, with no email required.
  • Ask us anything — including deletion of a purchase record. Write to the address below and we will answer.

Depending on where you live you may have additional rights over personal data (for example under the UK/EU GDPR or the CCPA). We will honour any such request we can verify; in most cases the honest answer will be that we hold nothing that identifies you.

Children

Astra is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will be noted in the app.
